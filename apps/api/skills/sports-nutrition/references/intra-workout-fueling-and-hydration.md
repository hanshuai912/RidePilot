# Intra-Workout Fueling & Hydration Strategies

Optimizing carbohydrate delivery, fluid intake, and electrolyte balance during training and competition delays fatigue, maintains central motor drive, and prevents performance degradation.

> [!NOTE]
> **Educational Disclaimer**: Intra-workout carbohydrate and fluid targets must be individualized through training practice to establish gut comfort and proper tolerance.

---

## 1. Hourly Carbohydrate Ingestion Protocol

Carbohydrate absorption is limited by intestinal transport capacity. Glucose relies on the sodium-dependent transporter SGLT1 (saturated at ~60 g/h), whereas fructose uses the independent transporter GLUT5 (saturated at ~30–50 g/h).

| Duration of Exercise | Hourly CHO Target | Recommended Carbohydrate Blend | Primary Physiological Mechanism |
| -------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **$< 45\text{ min}$** | None required | N/A | Endogenous glycogen stores fully sufficient |
| **45 – 75 min** | Mouth rinse or up to 30 g/h | Single transporter (Glucose/Maltodextrin) | Central nervous system stimulation via oral CHO receptors |
| **1 – 2.5 hours** | **30 – 60 g/h** | Single transporter (Glucose/Maltodextrin) | Maintains blood glucose and spares liver glycogen |
| **$> 2.5\text{ hours}$** | **60 – 90 g/h** | **Dual Transporter** (2:1 Maltodextrin:Fructose or 1:0.8) | Simultaneous SGLT1 + GLUT5 intestinal transport |
| **Ultra-Endurance ($> 4\text{h}$)** | **90 – 120 g/h** | **Dual Transporter** (1:0.8 Maltodextrin:Fructose) | Maximum exogenous oxidation rate; requires extensive gut training |

---

## 2. Sweat Rate Calculation & Fluid Replacement

Dehydration exceeding 2% of body mass causes cardiovascular drift, elevated core temperature, and reduced cardiac output.

### Sweat Rate Formula

$$\text{Sweat Rate (L/hr)} = \frac{(\text{Pre-Exercise Mass (kg)} - \text{Post-Exercise Mass (kg)}) + \text{Fluid Consumed (L)} - \text{Urine Output (L)}}{\text{Exercise Duration (hours)}}$$

### Fluid Guidance

- Target replacement of **60 – 80%** of total sweat losses during exercise.
- Avoid over-drinking beyond 100% of weight loss to prevent Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia (EAH).

---

## 3. Sodium & Electrolyte Management

- **Sweat Sodium Concentration**: Varies widely among athletes ($300\text{--}1,500\text{ mg Sodium/L sweat}$).
- **Target Replacement**: Consume **300 – 1,000 mg Sodium per hour** during events lasting longer than 90 minutes or in hot/humid environments.
- Sodium enhances intestinal glucose absorption via SGLT1 co-transport and helps maintain extracellular fluid volume and thirst drive.

---

## 4. Gut Training Protocol for High-Carbohydrate Intake

Ingesting $> 60\text{ g/h}$ of carbohydrates during intense exercise can induce gastrointestinal distress (bloating, nausea, cramping) if the intestinal tract is not adapted.

### 4-Week Gut Adaptation Progression

1. **Week 1–2**: Increase intra-workout CHO to 60 g/h on all long sessions using a 2:1 glucose:fructose mix. Consistently consume fluid with gels.
2. **Week 3**: Elevate target to 75–90 g/h during race-pace tempo blocks. Increase liquid volume to match gel concentration ($6\text{--}8\%\text{ carbohydrate solution}$).
3. **Week 4**: Test peak race-day target (90–100 g/h) during a simulated long workout. Evaluate GI comfort, stool consistency, and energy stability.
